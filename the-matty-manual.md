## (aka) About Me

<img src="{{"assets/images/matty-avatar.png" | relative_url}}" alt="Avator" class="left">
Hi, I'm Matt (my friends call me Matty).  Below is a little bit about me, what I value, and my preferences around work.  My hope is that it will provide a little insight as we start to work together and promote conversation about how to best communicate and collaborate.

## Values

- Agency - I value my own and treat yours with respect
- Logic and clear lines of reasoning - I value solutions that include a broader context of their environment/problem space.  I work best when I understand our approach and end goals(both near and long term) through [logical line](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Logical_reasoning) of reasoning.
- Transparency - Visible, explicit, and accurate communication. Lets us all not guess as much.
- Collaboration - I enjoy working through problems in real time with others. I deeply believe that many perspectives produce better results and the shared ownership of problems and their solutions has many benefits.
- Friendliness - I prefer to keep conversation light and friendly. Sometimes, the stakes are high, and environments are stressful. Let's not add to that stress if we can help it.
  - no yelling
  - no blaming.
  - Lets slow down when things get tense

## How those values play out

- I prefer real-time communication.
- I love pairing. Its useful darn near everywhere and not just for programming
- I do my best to choose my words carefully. I say what I mean, mean what I say, and try my hardest not to insinuate anything.
- Please be friendly but blunt. How we collaborate is itself a collaboration, and we won't get it right the first time or without work.
- I work as transparently as possible. I share work very, very early. Almost always before it's normally ready to be accepted. The first time you are likely seeing something I'm looking for your thoughts and am not suggesting we throw it straight out into the world. Please let me know if this is overwhelming or distracting.
- I examine problems - This can lead to slightly longer feedback cycles in some ways. Pausing and taking a small step back to think can help us all avoid a ton of additional effort later. I like to do this before we start 'the making' and periodically along the way. 
- I ask a lot of questions.  I likely have an answer to them many times. Your answers and perspectives are super important in helping us choose an approach and direction that works for both of us.
- I believe in context, so I can sometimes be a little verbose.

## Technical Details

Are you a programmer that is interested in how I think about the details related to making stuff? You are in luck. You have found the right section! Here are some ideas and reference 

I see the details around the bits mainly through the lenses of...
* [The 4 simple rules of design](https://blog.jbrains.ca/permalink/the-four-elements-of-simple-design).
* [Many More Much Smaller Steps](https://www.geepawhill.org/2021/09/29/many-more-much-smaller-steps-first-sketch/)
* Starting with the simplest thing that works.

I see the process of making stuff through the lenses of
* Delivering smaller amounts of value sooner and more regularly

* [Sustainable pace](https://ronjeffries.com/xprog/articles/jatsustainablepace/) 

  >  The XP Practice “Sustainable Pace” suggests that the team should work at a pace which produces a steady flow of results, su;stained indefinitely

* Ensuring the system(in our case the collection of people and interactions of which produce the thing we are making) is one that regularly learns from itself **and remembers** .

* Small 'p' plans.  Something quick, small, and easily adapts the inevitable changes through out a project. But a plan non-the-less.
